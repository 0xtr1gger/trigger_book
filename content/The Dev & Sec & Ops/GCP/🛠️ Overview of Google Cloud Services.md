## The Google Cloud network

- Google Cloud runs on Google's own global network. It's the largest network of its kind. 
- It's a high-thoughput network designed to minimize latency.
- It uses more than 100 content caching nodes worldwide. These are locations where frequently requested content is cached locally for quicker access.

---

- Google Cloud’s infrastructure is based in seven major geographic locations: North America, South America, Europe, Africa, the Middle East, Asia, and Australia.
- Each of these locations is divided into several different *regions* and *zones*.
- **Regions** represent independent geographic areas and are composed of zones. 
	- For example, London, or `europe-west2`, is a region that currently comprises three different zones. 
- A **zone** is an area where Google Cloud resources are deployed. 
---
- Some of Google Cloud’s services support placing resources in what is called a *multi-region*.
- You can find an up-to-date map of Google Cloud locations and zones at cloud.google.com/about/locations.
