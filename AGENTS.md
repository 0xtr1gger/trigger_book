## Vault laws

// written by a human, for AIs and people.

1. Any technical facts given must be valid and verifiable. There must be no false claims or such that can't be checked, unless explicitly listed or spoken of as a hypothesis/assumption/guess/probable but not guaranteed version.

2. No fluff. Technical notes must contain no conversational fluff or figurative speech. The goal is to deliver technically-dense information in a way that is easy to learn from and use in practice.

3. All commands must be valid, all options must be existing. Use one-line commands, when practical, to make copy-paste easier.

4. General flow of each article from beginning to end (not strict and more for a reference; should be kept in mind but can be modified for specific notes):
    - General -> details; important, core information -> less important. 
    - The core of the article (usually the practical sections, commands) must be somewhere at the top of the note, after the section explaining the technique. Resist the anti-pattern: numberous/lengthy theoretical sections, walls of text before the actial commands. This is important because the reader who came for practical stuff will simply get tired scrolling; at the same time, learning and refreshing requires explanations of how the subject works, therefore explanatory section must go first (such as in AS-REP roasting article).


- When editing articles, treat "//" as instructions
